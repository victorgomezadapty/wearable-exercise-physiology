What does a wrist wearable really see during stress and exercise?

An analysis of heart rate, electrodermal activity, skin temperature and movement recorded with an Empatica E4 during acute psychological stress, aerobic cycling and anaerobic sprints. Physiology comes first, every result is computed per participant, and machine learning is only evaluated on people it has never seen.

The question

Physical activity is not the same as physiological state

An accelerometer tells us that someone is moving. Heart rate, electrodermal activity and skin temperature tell us how the body is responding. The main question: how do wearable physiological signals differ between aerobic exercise, anaerobic exercise and acute stress? A follow-up runs through the whole project: how much of any difference is physiology, and how much is simply movement?

Movement

3-axis accelerometer (32 Hz). Context: pedalling rhythm, sprint bursts, stillness.

Cardiovascular

Wrist PPG (64 Hz) → heart rate and beat intervals: load, sympathovagal balance, HRV.

Autonomic & thermal

Electrodermal activity (sympathetic sudomotor drive, sweating) and skin temperature (peripheral perfusion).

Data & protocol

Three protocols, mostly the same people

PhysioNet Wearable Device Dataset from Induced Stress and Structured Exercise Sessions (v1.0.1; Hongn et al., Scientific Data 2025). 30 participants completed all three protocols, so most comparisons are within the same person. Every one of the 707 files was verified against the official checksums.

Acute stress

Seated. Baseline → Stroop (v1) → Trier Mental Challenge Test (timed arithmetic with aversive noise) → rest → opinion, opposite opinion and serial-subtraction tasks.

Aerobic

Incremental stationary cycling, 60 → 110 rpm with rising resistance (individual sessions), or a 70–95 rpm group spinning class.

Anaerobic

Wingate-style: 3–4 all-out sprints of 30–45 s, each followed by ~4 min of unloaded pedalling.

What Phase 1 uncovered. 25 data issues, 15 of them not listed by the authors: skin-temperature fault codes (207–235 °C), a stuck accelerometer axis, a session without usable PPG, tags out of order, and group-session timing differences. Also: no exercise session contains a true resting baseline. The only seated reference is on the stress day.
Signal quality first

Before any comparison: which windows can be trusted?

Empatica's heart rate was often physiologically implausible during cycling: flat, erratic, or locked onto the pedalling rhythm. Every 30-s window got a quality verdict per signal. The rules use signal properties only: PPG spectral quality, agreement between independent HR estimates, and cadence lock-in detected with the accelerometer. They never use the expected result.

Physiology

Stress and exercise both activate the body, in very different ways

All numbers are participant-level: windows are averaged within each person first, and the differences are paired within person, with bootstrap 95% CIs.

+5.0 bpm

Heart-rate response to the mental-stress task (95% CI 2.2–7.8, same day, n = 34). HR then drops below the starting baseline during recovery, which suggests the baseline itself held anticipatory arousal.

−0.006 g

Wrist movement decreases during stress. The physiological response happens with the body stiller than at rest, so it cannot be a movement artefact.

+49 bpm

At the aerobic peak, about 10× the stress response. EDA is ~11× its resting level, against ~1.6× under stress.

~60 s

After a sprint, HR keeps climbing. The cardiovascular response lags the mechanical effort, and valid HR from the wrist only returns about a minute after the sprint.

Self-report vs physiology. Within each person, blocks rated as more stressful also show higher HR (rrm = 0.40) and EDA (rrm = 0.18). The wearable tracks perceived stress, but only moderately. RMSSD fell by ~7 ms during the stress task (vagal withdrawal), but only 17–23 participants had enough clean beats, so this evidence is suggestive.
Machine learning, last

Can the three states be told apart in someone the model has never seen?

Classes: stress tasks, aerobic stages, and sprints plus their first 2 min of recovery. Validation: leave-one-subject-out. Metrics: macro-F1 and balanced accuracy, with CIs from resampling participants.

The honest message. This is not "AI detects stress and exercise with high accuracy". A wrist wearable separates seated stress from cycling mostly by movement. It detects acute stress while sitting from physiology with modest accuracy, and it struggles to tell two kinds of cycling apart. Feature importance is reported, but never read as physiological causation.
Method

Seven notebooks, one rule: physiology before modelling

  1. Data understanding. Checksums, sampling rates verified from headers, tag-to-protocol maps, a 25-item issue registry.
  2. Signal quality. Per-window rules for PPG/HR, EDA, temperature and ACC, calibrated on clean seated data.
  3. Protocol segmentation. Cadence fidelity checked with the accelerometer; analysis states comparable across protocol versions.
  4. Exploratory physiology. Event-locked responses, stage profiles, individual trajectories, multimodal profile.
  5. Feature engineering. 14 window features with a physiological rationale; univariate effects at participant level.
  6. Statistics. Paired effect sizes with bootstrap CIs, mixed models, ICCs, repeated-measures correlation, robustness checks.
  7. Classification. LOSO validation, a leakage demonstration, modality ablation, a missingness-shortcut test, permutation importance.
Limitations

What this dataset cannot tell us

Small, confounded sample

36 young, healthy adults. Cohort is confounded with sex, age, training status and protocol version (v1: 18 men; v2: 17 women + 1 man).

No reference measurements

No ECG, power output, VO₂ or lactate. "Aerobic" and "anaerobic" are protocol labels, not verified metabolic states.

Wrist PPG during exercise

HR is unmeasurable during sprints and often corrupted while pedalling. HRV is only defensible while seated.

Different days, short tasks

The resting reference is on another day. The 30-s stress tasks are short relative to EDA and HRV dynamics.

Nothing here supports clinical inference or diagnosis.
